About Me

I am a theoretical ecologist interested in understanding the genesis, maintenance, and consequences of biodiversity. Darwin's metaphor of the *entangled bank* beautifully captures the intricate web of interactions among organisms that underpins the natural world. Biodiversity shapes not only the richness and composition of ecological communities but also the nature and strength of interactions among species. These interactions, in turn, govern community dynamics and ultimately determine ecosystem properties such as productivity and stability. My research seeks to unravel how species interactions mediate the relationship between biodiversity and ecosystem functioning. To this end, I combine empirical data with mechanistic modelling and simulations, drawing on ecological theory to understand the processes underlying biodiversity–ecosystem functioning(BEF) relationships. My current work focuses on plant communities, particularly how plant–plant interactions shape ecosystem functioning and responses to environmental change. Looking ahead, I aim to extend this mechanistic perspective to broader ecological networks, incorporating trophic interactions to better understand the functioning and stability of complex ecological communities.

On-going Projects

Forest spatial arrangements against drought

The interactions pattern among trees may offer buffering effect against drought, which increases in frequence and intensity due to climate change. Finding the optimal planting arrangement for buffering drought effects thus is crucial to safeguard the ecosystem function as well as ecosystem servies forests provide

Tree diversity and temporal stability

Diversity can ensure the stable produciton of biomass in grassland communities, measured as the inverse of temporal variability. However, this effect in forests is less studied and the underlying mechanisms are not well understood. This project investigate tree diversity's effect on temporal stability through the lense of species interactions
